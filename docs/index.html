    Learn FreeFlow in 15 minutes

    FreeFlow turns your voice into text in any app on your Mac. Hold a key, talk, let go, and your words appear wherever you were typing. This page lets you try every feature in your browser before you install anything.

    Free speech recognition

    Transcription runs on your Mac with Apple's speech engine. No subscription.

    Claude when you want it

    Add one key and Claude cleans up what you said, for about $0.0002 a dictation.

    Works everywhere

    Mail, Slack, docs, your code editor, a browser. If you can type there, you can talk there.

    Meeting notes

    Live transcripts of calls and lectures, with a summary and your to-dos at the end.

    How this tutorial works

    Most lessons have a small pretend Mac. On a real Mac you'll use the fn key (it may show a 🌐 globe). Browsers can't see that key, so here you hold F instead. control, option, space and esc are the real keys. On a phone or tablet, press and hold the on-screen keys.

    • Press and hold F (or the on-screen fn key above) to check that it works

    Nothing here listens to you. The "speech" in this tutorial is scripted, and nothing you do is sent anywhere. Your progress is saved in this browser only.

    Your keys work. On to installing FreeFlow.

    Install FreeFlow

    FreeFlow is built from source on your Mac. It takes about five minutes. You can also skip ahead and finish the tutorial in your browser first.

    What you need

    • A Mac running macOS 26 or later
    • Xcode 26, free from the Mac App Store. Open it once after installing so it can finish setting up.
    • An Anthropic API key from console.anthropic.com, for the Claude features. Plain dictation works without one.

    1. Get the code

    Download FreeFlow (ZIP)

    Open the ZIP in Finder to unpack it. Then open Terminal (in Applications → Utilities) and run:

    cd ~/Downloads/FreeFlow-main
    git clone https://github.com/brian-stockwell/FreeFlow.git
cd FreeFlow

    2. Build and launch

    scripts/build.sh --run

    This builds the app, signs it, installs it to the Applications folder in your home folder and opens it. A waveform icon appears in your menu bar. FreeFlow lives there, not in the Dock.

    Tip: the build signs FreeFlow with your Apple Development certificate if you have one. That way macOS remembers your permissions when you update. To get one, open Xcode → Settings → Accounts, add your Apple ID, then choose Manage Certificates → + → Apple Development. A free Apple ID works. Without a certificate FreeFlow still runs, but macOS asks for its permissions again after every rebuild.

    3. Finish setup

    On first launch FreeFlow opens Settings. Work down the Setup section until every row has a green check:

    RowWhat to doWhy
    MicrophoneClick AllowSo it can hear you
    AccessibilityOpen System Settings and switch FreeFlow onFor the hotkeys, and to paste into the right text field
    Input MonitoringOpen System Settings and switch FreeFlow onTo notice when you press fn
    Speech modelClick DownloadApple's on-device model for your language
    Claude API keyPaste it, then click Test keyStored in your Mac's Keychain, never in plain text

    Coming from Wispr Flow? FreeFlow imports your Wispr dictionary, snippets, writing styles and email signature the first time it starts.

    • FreeFlow is built and its waveform icon is in my menu bar (click to check off)
    • Every row in Settings → Setup is green (click to check off)
    FreeFlow is installed. Time for your first dictation.

    Your first dictation

    Click into any text field, hold fn, say something and let go. FreeFlow pastes exactly what it heard.

    Plain fn uses Apple's speech recognition on your Mac, so it's free, private and quick. It types every word you say, though, including the ums. Lesson 4 fixes that.

    • Hold F (your stand-in for fn) until the sentence finishes, then let go
    • Tap F quickly. Nothing is pasted, because FreeFlow ignores accidental taps.

    Watch the bottom of the screen. That dark pill is the Flow Bar. It grows while FreeFlow listens and shows the words it's hearing.

    That's push-to-talk. Next: let Claude tidy it up.

    Let Claude clean it up

    Hold fn + control instead, and FreeFlow sends the transcript to Claude before pasting.

    Claude removes fillers and stutters, fixes grammar and punctuation, writes numbers the way you'd type them and turns spoken lists into real lists. It keeps your words and your voice. It doesn't rewrite you.

    • Hold control + F and let the sentence finish
    • Pick "coffee at two, actually three" and dictate it with control. Only your correction is kept.
    • Pick "first, second, third" and dictate it with control to get a numbered list
    • Start with F alone, then tap control while you're still talking

    Changed your mind mid-sentence? Say "actually", "no wait", "scratch that" or just restate it. FreeFlow keeps only the corrected version. "I actually loved it" stays as it is.

    Never lose a dictation. If Claude can't be reached, FreeFlow pastes the plain transcript instead and tells you.

    Nice. Next: how FreeFlow adapts to the app you're in.

    It knows where you're typing

    FreeFlow checks which app you're in, and in a browser which website, then formats your words to fit.

    Emails get a greeting and a sign-off. Chat messages stay short. It also reads the text around your cursor and names on screen, so it can continue your sentence and spell the people you're writing to correctly. Use the Dock to switch apps.

    • Dictate the message into Mail with control + F
    • Switch to Slack or Messages in the Dock and dictate the same thing

    Next: choose the tone for each kind of app.

    Pick a writing style

    Each category has its own tone, set on FreeFlow's Style page. Change one below and watch the same sentence change.

    You said: "sounds good see you at three tomorrow"

    • Change any category's style

    The Style page also has your email signature, which FreeFlow uses when it signs off an email, and a cleanup level. Light, the default, removes fillers and fixes grammar. Medium also tightens wordy sentences. None just punctuates.

    Next: talking without holding a key.

    Hands-free and cancel

    For longer thoughts you don't have to keep holding the key. FreeFlow keeps listening until you tap fn again.

    Start hands-free with fn + space, or double-tap fn. Press esc at any point to throw a recording away.

    • Hold F, press space, let go of both and keep "talking". Tap F when you're done.
    • Double-tap F (or the on-screen fn key) to start hands-free, then tap it again to finish
    • Start any recording and press esc to cancel it

    Good to know: a dictation stops by itself after 20 minutes, with a warning a minute before. You can change the limit in Settings.

    Next: editing text with your voice.

    Command Mode: edit with your voice

    Select some text, hold fn + option, and say what you want changed.

    FreeFlow sends your instruction and the selected text to Claude, then swaps in the result. With nothing selected, it works on the whole text field. Click the text in the window to select it.

    • Select the text, then hold option + F and give an instruction
    • Try a second instruction. Click Reset text first if you like.

    Already talking? Tap option while holding fn to switch into Command Mode mid-sentence. If no text field is active, the result is copied to your clipboard instead.

    Next: one-key rewrites.

    Transforms: one-key rewrites

    Transforms are saved instructions on a shortcut. Select text, press one, and the result replaces it.

    With nothing selected, a Transform rewrites the whole field. In an empty field it works on your last dictation from the past minute. You can edit these and create your own on FreeFlow's Transforms page. Here, press option + a number, or click a button.

    • Press option + 1 to Polish the rambling text
    • Click Reset text, then try another Transform (option + 2 to 5)
    Next: teach FreeFlow your words.

    Dictionary and snippets

    Teach FreeFlow the names, brands and jargon it gets wrong.

    A word makes sure something is always spelled your way. A replacement turns what the speech engine hears into what you meant. A snippet expands a short phrase you say into longer text, like your address or a meeting link. Try it below. The test box shows what FreeFlow would type.

    • Add a word or replacement of your own
    • Add a snippet, then type its trigger phrase into the test box

    It also learns on its own. If you fix a name or an unusual word right after FreeFlow pastes it, FreeFlow notices and adds that word to your dictionary.

    Next: meeting notes.

    Meetings and lectures

    Press option + N to record a meeting, and again to stop.

    FreeFlow transcribes your microphone as Me and your Mac's sound (the other people on the call) as Them, live and on your Mac. When you stop, Claude writes a summary, the decisions and everyone's to-dos. In a class? Tap control on its own while recording to switch to Lecture mode, which writes study notes instead.

    • Start a meeting with option + N (or the button), let a few lines come in, then stop it
    • While recording, tap control on its own to switch to Lecture mode
    • After the summary appears, ask a question about the meeting

    Private by design: meeting audio is deleted as soon as the transcript is saved. FreeFlow can also notice when you join a Zoom, Teams, Meet, Webex or FaceTime call and offer to record it.

    Next: the FreeFlow window and what this all costs.

    Your FreeFlow window

    Click the waveform icon in the menu bar and choose Open FreeFlow.

    Paste it again

    Clicked away before the paste landed? ⌘ + control + V pastes your last transcript again, and ⌘ + control + C copies it.

    • Paste your last transcript with ⌘ + control + V (or the on-screen key)
